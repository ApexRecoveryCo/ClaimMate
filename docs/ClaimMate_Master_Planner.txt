1. Executive Strategy Snapshot
ClaimMate should be built as a mobile-first web app that turns a stressful insurance, damage or disaster situation into a structured evidence file. The strongest first version is not a broad insurance platform. It is a focused claim organiser that guides the user through a messy event, helps them collect the right evidence, tracks communication, and exports a claim pack.
Decision area
Planner recommendation
Product category
Evidence organisation, claim preparation and disaster documentation app.
Primary launch market
Australia-first, because the evidence base is strong and the regulatory/environmental context is clear. Expand later to New Zealand, Canada, United Kingdom and United States with localised wording.
Primary user
Everyday person dealing with property damage, vehicle damage, theft, severe weather, water damage, fire or a slow insurer response.
Core value moment
User creates one claim, uploads evidence, adds call notes, and receives a clean AI-generated claim summary and evidence pack.
First paid feature
Exportable AI evidence pack for one claim.
AI role
Structure messy information, summarise documents, identify evidence gaps, draft communications and generate timeline-based summaries.
Non-negotiable safety boundary
No legal advice, no financial advice, no emergency instruction, no guarantee of claim success, no insurer decision prediction.
Build approach
Next.js + Supabase + Claude API + PDF export. Start as a mobile-friendly web app before native mobile.
1.1 North Star
North Star statement
Help a user go from "I do not know what to do or what proof I need" to "I have a clear claim file, organised evidence, a timeline and a professional summary I can use."
1.2 What ClaimMate must become
A calm command centre for claims, evidence and disaster documentation.
A personal memory system for what happened, who said what, and what evidence exists.
A structured vault for photos, videos, receipts, quotes, reports, policy documents, emails and call notes.
A plain-English assistant that helps users understand their own documents without pretending to be a lawyer or insurer.
A PDF evidence pack generator that saves hours of manual admin.
1.3 What ClaimMate must avoid
Avoid positioning as an insurance advice tool.
Avoid promising that a claim will be accepted.
Avoid saying an insurer is wrong unless the user uploads evidence and the system frames it as a document inconsistency or question to raise.
Avoid collecting more sensitive data than needed.
Avoid becoming a generic storage app. The product must stay outcome-focused: claims, evidence and timelines.
2. Evidence Base and Market Rationale
The rationale for ClaimMate is based on a simple market signal: insurance and disaster events are high-stress, evidence-heavy, deadline-driven and financially important. Users often need better records, not more information overload.
Evidence signal
Data point
Product implication
General insurance complaints
AFCA received 34,231 general insurance complaints in 2024-25, up 17% from the previous year. [S1]
There is a large and growing need for users to keep clean records and escalate clearly when claims become difficult.
Extreme weather claim volume
Extreme weather generated $4.8 billion in insured losses in 2025, according to ICA updated analysis released in April 2026. [S2]
Severe weather is not rare enough to ignore. ClaimMate should support storm, hail, flood, cyclone and water damage from day one.
Preparedness gap
Australian Red Cross reported that 90% of Australians had been affected by an emergency or disaster, but only 35% were prepared. [S3]
The app should work before disaster, not only after. Home inventory and preparedness checklists are essential later-stage features.
Complaint handling gap
ASIC found insurers were failing to identify one in six customer complaints. [S4]
ClaimMate should help users recognise when a concern may need to be framed as a complaint and preserve a clear timeline.
Cash settlements and expert reports
ASIC said home insurers need better oversight of independent experts and clearer communication around cash settlements. [S5]
ClaimMate should help users compare insurer responses, expert reports, quotes and settlement questions in plain English.
Government resilience investment
The Australian Government Disaster Ready Fund provides up to $1 billion over five years for resilience and risk reduction. [S6]
Disaster readiness is a national priority. ClaimMate can position around preparedness, documentation and recovery admin.
2.1 The evidence-backed product gap
The market already has insurer portals, emergency apps, cloud drives, note-taking apps and generic document scanners. The gap is a user-owned claim operating system. Existing tools store information. ClaimMate should organise information into a claim-ready structure.
Current workaround
Where it fails

Photo capture with categories, date, claim link, AI caption and evidence checklist.

Dedicated claim timeline and document list.

Emergency readiness app

2.2 Wedge strategy
Best wedge
Start with one urgent use case: "I have damage and need to organise my claim." After proving that, expand into home inventory, disaster readiness, renewal records, family sharing and B2B support tools.

8.2 Mobile-first UX principles
Each screen must have one main action.
Use plain-English labels such as "Add photos" instead of "Upload assets".
Use progress language: "3 of 7 evidence items added".
Use calm colours and high contrast.
Avoid dense paragraphs during claim creation. Use short questions and examples.
Give users a "skip for now" option so they can keep moving.
Show safety copy where relevant: "If there is immediate danger, contact emergency services."
Make the export button visible only when enough minimum information exists.

9.4 Architecture notes
Use Supabase Row Level Security from day one. Every claim, evidence item, log and AI output must be scoped to user_id.
Process AI requests server-side. Do not expose Claude API keys to the browser.
Use signed URLs for private file access.
Limit file types and file sizes in MVP to reduce abuse and cost.
Create AI prompt versions so outputs can be debugged and improved.
Keep uploaded original files untouched. AI summaries should be separate editable metadata.
Build export pack as a server-side job if generation becomes slow.
10. Claude AI System Design
10.1 AI principles
Principle
Meaning in ClaimMate
Do not invent facts
AI must only use user-entered claim facts and uploaded document summaries. If information is missing, it must say so.
Use cautious language
Say "may", "consider", "ask your insurer", "based on the information provided".
No legal/financial advice
AI can organise, summarise and draft. It cannot advise on rights, coverage outcome or legal strategy.
Make outputs editable
Every AI output should be a draft the user can review and change.
Show source basis
Where possible, outputs should list which claim notes/evidence items were used.
Plain English first
Avoid insurance jargon or explain it.

11. Security, Privacy and Trust
ClaimMate will hold sensitive information: damage photos, policy documents, addresses, claim numbers, receipts, personal communications and possibly health or family information. Trust cannot be bolted on later.
11.1 Trust requirements
Requirement
Planner instruction
Data minimisation
Collect only what is needed to organise a claim. Make optional fields truly optional.
Private-by-default storage
No public access to evidence files. Use signed URLs and RLS.
User control
Users can export evidence packs and request deletion.
Clear AI disclosure
Every AI output should be labelled as AI-generated draft for user review.
Emergency limitation
Show clear notice that ClaimMate does not replace emergency services.
Sensitive file handling
Limit file types, scan metadata where possible, and prevent public indexing.
Retention policy
Let users know how long data is stored and how they can delete it.
Incident response
Prepare a breach response and user notification process before launch.
11.2 Legal and compliance boundaries
Use disclaimers, but do not rely on disclaimers alone. Design prompts and UI so the product does not provide regulated advice.
Do not say "your insurer must pay" or "this is covered". Say "the policy text appears to say... confirm with your insurer or adviser."
Do not tell users to enter unsafe buildings or delay emergency action to collect evidence.
When explaining policy documents, focus on summaries and questions to ask, not decisions.
For complaints, produce factual chronology and document lists rather than legal arguments.
Consider legal review before launching paid claim pack, policy explainer or complaint summary features.

20. Verified Policy Knowledge Base and Insurer Policy Backend
This section turns ClaimMate from a generic AI document organiser into a trusted policy-aware claim preparation system. The product decision is that ClaimMate must not answer insurance cover questions from general AI knowledge. It must answer only from the customer’s uploaded documents and/or a verified, version-controlled policy library stored in the ClaimMate backend.
20.1 Product decision
ClaimMate will maintain a verified policy knowledge base for insurer documents that affect claim preparation.
Claude will be used as an explanation and drafting layer, not as the source of truth.
The source of truth will be the retrieved policy document, policy schedule, clause text, page reference and effective date.
If ClaimMate cannot match the customer to the correct policy version, it must say that more information is needed.
The app will use cautious language: “may be covered”, “may apply”, “check your Policy Schedule”, and “ask the insurer to explain how this clause was applied”.
The app will not say a customer is definitely covered, that an insurer must pay, or that a complaint will succeed.
20.2 Documents ClaimMate should store
The backend must store more than one “policy” PDF. Australian insurance products commonly rely on a group of documents that work together. The user’s Policy Schedule or Certificate of Insurance is critical because it confirms the individual cover, excess, insured address, sums insured and optional extras.

20.6 Policy version matching rules
Policy version matching is a core trust feature. ClaimMate must not assume that the latest PDS on an insurer website applies to a customer’s claim. A customer may be on an older PDS, an older renewal version, or a PDS modified by an SPDS.
Ask the user for insurer, product name, policy start date, renewal date and claim date.
Ask the user to upload the Certificate of Insurance or Policy Schedule before answering detailed cover questions.
Match policy documents using insurer, product type, effective date range, state availability and document title.
If multiple documents match, show the likely matches and ask the user to confirm the document title or upload their schedule.
Keep old documents archived and searchable. Never overwrite an old PDS with a new version.
Link SPDS documents to the PDS they modify. If an SPDS exists in the date range, retrieve it with the PDS.
Use the claim date and policy period to retrieve the relevant policy version.
If ClaimMate cannot determine the version, it can still organise evidence but must not explain cover as if the policy is confirmed.
20.7 RAG answer workflow
ClaimMate should use Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG). The backend retrieves the relevant approved clauses first, then Claude explains them in plain English. Claude should never be asked to answer from memory about insurer cover.
User asks a policy or coverage question.
App identifies claim, insurer, product, policy period and document version.
App retrieves relevant approved clauses from policy_clauses and the user’s uploaded policy schedule where available.
App retrieves related clauses: definitions, exclusions, evidence requirements, claim conditions and complaint clauses.
Claude receives only the retrieved text, customer facts needed for context, and the required answer format.
Claude answers cautiously in plain English and includes document, section and page references.
App stores the answer, source clauses and confidence score in ai_outputs and policy_answer_sources.
If sources are missing, Claude must say more information is needed.
20.8 User-facing answer format
Every policy explanation should be source-backed and structured the same way so users learn to trust the format. The answer should avoid firm outcome promises.
Answer section
Required content
Plain-English answer
A cautious summary of what the retrieved clause says. Use “may”, “appears”, “based on this document”, and “if conditions are met”.
What this may mean for your claim
Practical explanation of how the clause could relate to the user’s situation, without deciding the claim outcome.
Evidence you may want to collect
Photos, receipts, reports, quotes, call logs or other documents linked to the clause and claim type.
Important limits or exclusions
Relevant exclusions, limits, conditions or missing schedule details.
Source used
Insurer, product, document title, effective date, section title, page number and clause category.
Confidence and limitation

20.12 Monthly update workflow
Policy documents change. A backend policy library is only useful if it stays current and keeps historical versions. The monthly check should be treated as an operational process, not a one-off setup task.
Maintain a source URL list for every insurer product document page.
Check document URLs monthly for new file names, new versions or changed PDFs.
Download current PDFs and compare file hashes against the stored version.
If the hash changed, ingest the new document as a new version.
Do not delete the old version. Mark it archived or superseded with effective_to date where known.
Run extraction, classification, embedding and human review on the new version.
Create a change note summarising what changed between versions where possible.
Retest top customer questions against the new version.
Update product availability and document status.
Log last_checked_at for auditability.

20.14 Claude policy-backend build prompt
We need to add a verified Policy Knowledge Base to ClaimMate. The app must not answer insurance coverage questions from general AI knowledge. It must only answer using retrieved policy clauses from approved policy documents stored in the backend or from the customer’s uploaded policy documents. Build the backend architecture for insurers, insurance products, policy documents, policy clauses, customer policies, policy version matching, policy document ingestion, AI clause extraction, human review and approval, and RAG-based customer answers with source citations. Use Supabase Postgres, Supabase Storage, pgvector, Next.js and Claude API. The system must store insurer brand name, legal insurer or underwriter, product name, policy type, document type, document version, issue date, effective dates, source URL, PDF file, file hash, extracted clauses, page numbers, clause categories, plain-English summaries and approval status. The AI assistant must retrieve relevant approved clauses, answer only from those clauses, show source document and page number, use cautious wording, avoid legal or financial advice, tell the user to upload their Policy Schedule when needed, and say when there is not enough information. Create database schema, Supabase migration SQL, admin dashboard plan, ingestion workflow, retrieval workflow, AI prompts, user-facing answer format, security rules, test plan and build steps.
20.15 Acceptance criteria for policy intelligence
A user can select an insurer and product when creating a claim.
A user can upload their Policy Schedule and link it to a claim.
An admin can add a PDS, SPDS or Key Facts Sheet with effective dates and source URL.
The system stores old policy versions and does not overwrite them.
The system extracts clauses with page numbers and section titles.
A human reviewer can approve or reject extracted clauses.
Claude can answer a policy question only when approved clauses are retrieved.
The answer includes insurer, product, document, effective date, page number, clause title and limitation wording.
If no source is available, the answer refuses to guess and asks the user to upload the required document.
The app logs which clauses were used in each AI answer.
The export pack can include policy clauses that support the customer’s questions, clearly marked as source-backed references.
Every policy answer uses cautious wording and avoids legal, financial or insurance advice.
